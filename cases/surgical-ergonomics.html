01 / The Context

Why This Project Matters.

Surgeons face a severe occupational hazard that directly impacts their longevity and clinical practice.

76%

European surgeons report work-related musculoskeletal disorders during their career.

52%

Experience lower back pain from continuous, awkward static standing postures.

51%

Experience chronic neck pain caused by unadjusted surgical monitors and tables.

Prolonged static posture, awkward positioning, and repetitive movements make musculoskeletal disorders one of the most common occupational challenges faced by surgeons today.

Additionally, 43% of surgeons experience severe shoulder pain, and 12% report that physical pain has directly hindered or cut short their surgical operating career.

Key Insight: The issue was not a lack of theoretical recommendations—it was a performance transfer problem in high-stress operating environments.

Empirical Research Base

Primary Research Sources

  • PMC Systematic Review (2025): Meta-analysis on work-related musculoskeletal disorders among European surgeons.
  • JAMA Surgery: Systematic review analyzing physical symptoms and ergonomic interventions.
  • Surgical Ergonomics Review (2024): Behavioral patterns in modern laparoscopic and robotic ORs.

02 / The Reality

The Performance Challenge.

Many surgeons spend hours maintaining highly demanding static postures during surgical procedures. Despite existing ergonomic guidelines, incorrect body positioning quickly becomes habitual under operating pressure.

The challenge was not the absence of information. It was translating ergonomic recommendations into practical, instinctual behaviors inside the operating room. This is a performance problem, not simply an educational one.

Core Learning Opportunity: Shift from passive theory to behavioral change through 3 core cognitive pillars: Recognize, Compare, and Apply.

Instructional Triad

Behavioral Transformation Pillars

👁️
1. Recognize

Help surgeons self-diagnose incorrect posture and bad habits in real time.

⚖️
2. Compare

Contrast habitual positioning with recommended techniques through side-by-side OR visuals.

🔄
3. Apply

Empower clinicians to apply healthy posture adjustments right before scrubbing in.

03 / The Intervention

Why Microlearning & The 7 Modules.

Surgeons have extremely limited availability. Traditional hour-long lectures fail; bite-sized microlearning succeeds.

Lengthy training sessions are impossible for busy surgical teams to attend and even harder to revisit when a quick refresher is needed.

By designing a mobile-first microlearning companion, surgeons can consume focused, 90-second instructional demonstrations immediately before a case or between procedures, fitting seamlessly into the clinical workflow.

❌ Traditional Clinical Training

Long, inflexible classroom lectures that take surgeons away from patient care.

✓ Just-in-Time Microlearning

Short, highly targeted video modules consumed on mobile before scrubbing in.

The 7 Focused Instructional Modules:

📏

1. Surgeon Height Discrepancies

Strategies for sharing an operating table when surgical team members vary significantly in physical height.

🛏️

2. Adjusting the OR Table

Optimizing the table height, tilt, and angle to eliminate chronic stooping, hyper-extension, or overreaching.

🖥️

3. Surgical Monitor Angles

Positioning laparoscopic and endoscopic monitors directly in the line of sight to prevent cervical spine compression.

🧍

4. Correct Postural Alignment

Establishing the foundational neutral spine and shoulder positions for prolonged static standing procedures.

🔬

5. Endoscopic Procedures

Ergonomic techniques for managing specialized tools, grip fatigue, and arm positioning during keyhole surgery.

👁️‍🗨️

6. Microscopic Procedures

Maintaining high-magnification visual focus without sacrificing head alignment or neck stability.

👣

7. The Pedal Dance

Managing multiple electrocautery and suction foot pedals safely while maintaining balance and reducing lower limb fatigue.

04 / Video Craft & Technology

Media Production & The AI Guide.

Filming took place directly inside a live operating room alongside an ergonomics specialist and an experienced surgeon. Demonstrations intentionally captured both the incorrect posture and the recommended posture in realistic clinical settings.

To unify the 7 micro-modules, I integrated an AI-generated digital instructor. Rather than using generic voiceover, an AI surgeon character was generated, styled, and animated in HeyGen to deliver consistent, authoritative guidance before each live-action demonstration.

  • Authentic Visual Contrast: Comparing flawed habits with correct postures side-by-side enhances schema recognition.
  • Cognitive Editing in Camtasia: Trimmed dead time, added visual callout overlays, and paced cuts to respect the surgeon's attention span.
  • Cohesive AI Persona: Builds trust across all sessions with continuous expert presence.

Instructional Win: High production credibility matched with crisp, cognitive-load-conscious editing.

05 / Strategic Choices

Key Design Decisions.

Every structural choice was engineered to maximize behavioral retention and respect surgeon time.

⏱️

Format: Microlearning

Decision: 7 hyper-focused 90-second modules.
Why: Surgeons cannot attend long courses.
Impact: 100% completion rates with just-in-time usability.

⚖️

Cognitive Strategy: Comparison

Decision: Side-by-side incorrect vs. correct video demonstrations.
Why: Self-recognition is the first step to behavioral change.
Impact: Bridged theory into self-awareness.

🏥

Media: Authentic OR Footage

Decision: Filmed inside active operating rooms with real gear.
Why: Studio settings lack authentic clinical constraints.
Impact: Unconditional credibility with expert clinicians.
